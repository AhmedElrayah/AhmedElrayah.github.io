Opening Statement

As a computer science student with a minor in Business Analytics, I am interested in how computational thinking can turn complex problems into practical solutions. I am drawn to work that requires both logical reasoning and an understanding of what people need from technology. My developing identity as a computer scientist centers on breaking problems into manageable parts, evaluating possible approaches, and building solutions that are clear, reliable, and useful. I want to understand not only how a program works, but also why a particular design is appropriate for the problem it addresses.

My interests include software development, working with data, and the relationship between technical decisions and organizational needs. These areas involve questions that extend beyond writing code: How should information be organized so that it can be used effectively? What makes a solution easy to test, maintain, and improve? How can a complicated process become simpler without losing important details? My computer science studies provide a foundation for approaching these questions systematically, while my BSA minor adds another perspective on the contexts in which technical solutions are used.

This portfolio brings together work that represents my development in problem solving, implementation, and technical communication. I have chosen these artifacts to show both what I can produce and how I approach a challenge. Together, they provide a basis for examining how I interpret requirements, organize ideas, make design choices, and evaluate results. Their value lies in the reasoning behind the finished work as well as in the outcomes themselves. A completed solution matters, but so does the ability to explain its structure, recognize its limitations, and identify what could be improved.

The portfolio also reflects my growing understanding that effective computing requires more than getting a program to run. A solution should address the intended problem, behave consistently, and remain understandable to someone reviewing or extending it. Clear explanations and careful evaluation are therefore part of the technical work. I aim to bring the same attention to communicating a solution that I bring to developing it.

As I look beyond graduation, I am interested in opportunities to apply my background to practical technical problems. Software development and work involving data or business systems are directions I would like to explore. My immediate goal is to deepen my technical skills, gain experience with larger projects, and learn from collaboration with others. I expect my professional focus to become more specific as I encounter new problems and see where my abilities can contribute most effectively.

One important lesson I have learned about myself as a thinker is the value of making my reasoning explicit. Breaking a difficult task into smaller steps helps me identify assumptions, locate errors, and decide what to try next. I am developing greater patience with revision and a stronger appreciation for the understanding that comes from examining an unsuccessful approach. This portfolio represents a stage in that continuing development: a foundation of technical knowledge, an increasingly deliberate approach to problem solving, and a commitment to learning through practice.
